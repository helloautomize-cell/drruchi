Cracked Tooth (Fractured Tooth): Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment
 A cracked tooth is more common than you might think — and the pain can show up suddenly, especially when chewing or drinking something cold. Depending on how deep the crack goes, the tooth may be mildly damaged or severely fractured. Understanding why a tooth cracks, what symptoms to look for, and how treatment works can help you prevent further damage. 
What is a Cracked Tooth?
 A cracked tooth means the tooth structure has developed a visible or invisible fracture. This can be a tiny hairline crack or a deep split running vertically. Unlike a chipped tooth, where a small piece breaks off, a cracked tooth involves a fracture within the actual tooth structure. Molars crack more often because of their chewing pressure. Causes of a Cracked Tooth
Biting into Hard Objects
 Hard foods like ice, nuts, or dried sweets can put too much pressure on the tooth. If the enamel is already weak, a sudden crack can develop instantly. 
Teeth Grinding (Bruxism)
 People who grind their teeth put constant pressure on the enamel. Over time, this friction weakens the tooth surface, increasing the chance of cracks. A night guard is often recommended to prevent further fractures. 
Trauma or Injury
 Sports accidents, falls, or sudden impacts can cause cracks in the front teeth or molars. Children may need a 
Pediatric Dentist
 check-up after trauma. 
Age-Related Wear and Tear
 Teeth naturally weaken with age. As enamel thins out, the tooth becomes more vulnerable to fractures. Routine 
Teeth Cleaning
 helps maintain enamel strength. 
Large Fillings or Cavities
 A tooth with a big filling has less natural structure to support pressure. This makes it easier for the surrounding walls to crack under chewing force. In such cases, a 
Dental Crown
 is often recommended. 
Temperature Changes
 Switching quickly from hot to cold foods (or vice versa) can cause the tooth to expand and contract, creating tiny cracks — especially in teeth with restorations. 
Symptoms of a Cracked Tooth
Pain while chewing:
 Pain that appears only while biting down and disappears afterward.
Sensitivity:
 Discomfort when eating hot, cold, or sweet foods.
Sharp pain:
 Sudden, stabbing pain that comes and goes.
Visible cracks:
 Some fractures may be visible to the eye or when illuminated.
Food discomfort:
 Crunchy or sticky foods may worsen the pain.
How to Fix a Cracked Tooth
 The treatment depends on the depth and location of the crack. Delaying treatment can worsen the fracture or cause infection. 
Dental Bonding
 For small cracks, composite bonding restores structure and prevents further cracking. It’s quick, painless, and ideal for surface fractures. 
Root Canal Treatment
 If the crack reaches the pulp (nerve chamber), it can cause severe pain or infection. A Root Canal Treatment removes damaged pulp and prevents tooth loss. Afterward, a crown is placed for full protection. 
Tooth Extraction
 If the crack extends below the gum line, saving the tooth may not be possible. In such cases, extraction followed by a Dental Implant is the best option. 
Dental Veneers & Smile Makeover
 For aesthetic restoration after damage, treatments like Dental Veneers or a  Smile Makeover can improve your smile. 
Final Thoughts
 A cracked tooth may start as minor discomfort, but it can quickly lead to serious dental complications if ignored. Whether your tooth needs bonding, a crown, a root canal, or extraction, early treatment ensures better results.

FAQs About Cracked Teeth
1. Can a broken tooth be fixed?
 Yes. Depending on the damage, it can be repaired with bonding, a crown, root canal therapy, or an implant after extraction. 
2. Why does my cracked tooth not hurt?
 If the crack hasn't reached the nerve, you may not feel pain. But the crack can worsen anytime, so a General Dentistry evaluation is important. 
3. Do I need to see a dentist for a broken tooth?
 Yes. Even painless cracks can lead to deeper fractures, infection, or tooth loss. An 
Emergency Dentistry
 visit may be needed if pain suddenly appears. 
4. What medicine is good for a cracked tooth?
 OTC pain relievers like ibuprofen help temporarily, but only a dentist can provide a permanent solution. Avoid 
Teeth Whitening
 products until treatment is done if sensitivity is present.
