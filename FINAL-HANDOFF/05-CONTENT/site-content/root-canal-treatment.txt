Signs You Need a Root Canal Treatment
If you're facing any discomfort in your inner tooth or pulp region, you need to have a root canal dentist take a closer look. If an infected pulp is left untreated, it can cause an abscess.
To avoid this, check out the symptoms and signs that indicate you need an RCT of teeth:
Toothache and gum issues
  You might suffer from excruciating pain in your tooth that can also spread to your jaw and face. Additionally, your gums might swell, or there can be pus accumulation.
Heat and cold sensitivity
  You may feel tingling or pain after ice cream or a cold beverage. If the pain stays for a while, you might need RCT.
Discolouration of the tooth
 can happen when the tooth has an infected pulp, and the subsequent poor blood supply can lead to discolouration.
Gum pimples
  You may observe a pimple on the gums.
Loose teeth
  An infected tooth might become loose as the pus softens the bone supporting your tooth.
You should consult an endodontist if you suffer from some or all of the above symptoms. Remember that getting your root canals checked by your root canal dentist early will prevent any additional surgeries later.

Preliminary Examination and Preparation
The general RCT process starts with the doctor examining the canals and administering a local anesthetic. This is done through an injection that numbs the area for the surgery. After that, the doctor positions a dental dam in your mouth and a protective sheet that separates your tooth, keeping it clean and saliva-free.
Cleaning and Shaping the Area
The doctor then drills a small hole in the tooth's surface, usually at the crown. He uses tiny instruments to access the root canals, cleaning the pulp chamber with an antibacterial solution and carefully removing the dead pulp to eliminate the infection. Next, the dentist starts shaping the root canals to fill them later. Once the area is clean, tiny instruments help the doctor make space for adding the filling substance.
Filling the Root Canals
After your root canal is cleaned and properly shaped, the dentist uses a biocompatible material, usually a substance called gutta-percha, which is heated and compressed to fit the walls of the tooth. After that, the doctor adds an adhesive cement to seal the area to keep the region free from bacteria and infection.
Providing Medication
Our dentist will provide suitable antibiotics and pain medications after completing the treatment procedure. It will kill any remaining bacteria in your root canals and help tackle post-surgical pain. Our experienced team also ensures maximum comfort and safety throughout the procedure.
Restoring the Crown
If there was a lot of damage to the back of the tooth where the treatment was performed, you might need to return for a crown restoration. In this process, our endodontic doctors will position the crown around the treated canal area to strengthen it. We ensure that the ground matches your existing teeth to maintain uniformity so you can chew your food without discomfort.

                     What is a root canal treatment, and why is it necessary? 
Root canal treatment, also called endodontic therapy, is used to treat infected or damaged tooth pulp. It's necessary when the pulp inside the tooth becomes infected or inflamed due to deep decay, trauma, or other issues. The procedure involves removing the infected tissue, cleaning the tooth's interior, and sealing it to prevent further infection.
                     How do I know if I need a root canal? 
Symptoms that may indicate the need for a root canal include severe tooth pain, especially when chewing, sensitivity to hot or cold, swelling or tenderness in the gums, a pimple-like bump on the gums near the affected tooth, or tooth discoloration. If you experience any of these, see a dentist for evaluation.
                     Is a root canal painful? 
Root canal treatments are typically not painful as they are done under local anesthesia to numb the tooth and surrounding areas. You might feel discomfort or soreness after the procedure, but this can be managed with over-the-counter pain medication and usually subsides within a few days.
                     How long does a root canal procedure take? 
The time needed for a root canal varies based on the tooth's location, the complexity of the root canal system, and the extent of infection. Generally, it takes one to two hours per tooth. Sometimes, multiple appointments are needed to complete the treatment.
                     What happens after a root canal? 
After the procedure, your dentist may prescribe antibiotics and recommend pain medication to manage discomfort. A follow-up appointment might be needed to check healing and discuss restoring the tooth with a crown. Maintaining good oral hygiene, including brushing, flossing, and regular dental check-ups, is crucial to keep the treated tooth healthy.
