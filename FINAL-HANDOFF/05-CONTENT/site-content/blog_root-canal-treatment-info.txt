What Is Root Canal Treatment?
 A Root Canal Treatment is a dental procedure that treats infections inside the tooth’s pulp — the soft core containing nerves and blood vessels. When this pulp becomes infected or inflamed due to deep decay, cracks, or trauma, it can cause intense pain and swelling. If left untreated, it could lead to abscesses or even tooth loss. Maintaining 
toothpaste habits
 can help prevent further problems. 
The primary goal of 
RCT
 is to save your natural tooth while eliminating pain and infection. Sometimes, post-treatment, a dental restoration may be needed, and you can check 
dental bridges cost
 options if necessary.
When Do You Need a Root Canal?
Persistent toothache or sharp pain when chewing
Sensitivity to hot or cold that lingers
Swelling or tenderness in the surrounding gums
Darkening or discoloration of the tooth
Recurring pimple or abscess on the gums (
mouth ulcers
)
💡 Tip:
 Early intervention can prevent more extensive treatments and reduce costs, sometimes avoiding more complex procedures later.
Step-by-Step: How Is Root Canal Treatment Done?
Diagnosis & X-rays:
 Your dentist starts with an X-ray to examine the tooth and check for infection in the surrounding bone.
Local Anesthesia:
 A local anesthetic is used to numb the area, ensuring a pain-free experience during the procedure.
Pulp Removal:
 An opening is made in the tooth to remove the infected pulp. Specialized tools clean the inner chambers.
Cleaning & Shaping the Canals:
 The root canals are cleaned, shaped and disinfected to eliminate any remaining bacteria.
Filling & Sealing:
 The cleaned canals are filled with a biocompatible material (usually gutta-percha) and sealed to prevent reinfection.
Restoration with Crown:
 A crown is often placed over the treated tooth to restore its function, strength and appearance.

Factors Affecting Root Canal Treatment Cost
Tooth Type:
 Molars typically have more canals, making them harder to treat — hence, they’re more expensive.
Dentist’s Expertise:
 A highly experienced endodontist may charge more but offers a higher success rate.
Technology Used:
 Laser-assisted RCTs and digital X-rays may add to the cost but enhance precision and reduce treatment time.
Material of the Crown:
 Zirconia crowns are more durable and aesthetic but come at a premium compared to metal or porcelain.
Complications or Additional Procedures:
 Bone grafting, post-core buildup, or treating deep abscesses can increase costs — sometimes avoiding the need for dental implants later.
