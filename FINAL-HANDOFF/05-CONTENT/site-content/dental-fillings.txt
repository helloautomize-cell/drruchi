Common Dental Filling Problems and Their Solutions
While a dental filling procedure generally avoids additional complications, the varying degrees of tooth decay in individual cases can present some issues for patients.
Persistent Ache & Hypersensitivity Post-Procedure
 Oral hypersensitivity and mild toothache are common after a dental filling but usually resolve within two to four weeks. During this period, it's advisable to avoid sugary or hard-to-chew foods. Persistent pain and sensitivity beyond this timeframe may occur due to improper cavity preparation, incorrectly shaped filling, or severe gum roots or tooth pulp infection. Dentists may recommend various treatments based on the underlying cause, including additional polishing for improperly shaped fillings or a 
root canal
 procedure for those with infected gum tissue or tooth pulp.
Allergic Reactions
Though uncommon, allergic reactions to dental fillings typically arise from metallic base materials. For example, amalgam fillings, which include mercury, can induce allergies, particularly in individuals with a personal or family history of metal allergies. Symptoms mimic skin allergies, featuring itchy skin, rashes, raised hives, facial swelling, and pain around the cheeks or eyes. Replacing dental amalgam fillings with other restorative materials is recommended in such cases.
Deteriorated Fillings
Constant pressure from grinding, chewing, or involuntary clenching can lead to the wearing out of fillings. In such cases, bacteria or food particles may penetrate beneath the cracked filling, resulting in additional dental damage. Additionally, newly placed fillings can dislodge due to incorrect application, bacterial contamination, or fractal damage.
Extensive decay or insufficient remaining tooth structure may necessitate a 
dental crown
 instead of a filling. Detecting damage to a cavity filling can be challenging for the average person, emphasizing the importance of regular dental check-ups to promptly identify and address such issues.

Types of Dental Fillings with Advantages & Disadvantages
Gold or Porcelain Fillings:
 Gold and porcelain fillings, owing to their base materials,boast high durability, often lasting 15 years or more, withminimal corrosion risk. Their robustness against constant chewing pressure reduces the likelihood of fractures or damage. However, drawbacks include the potential forgalvanic shock,causing sharp pain when interacting with other materials. It arises from the interaction between saliva and metal fillings, generating an electric current. Another drawback is theirexpensive and intricate application process, spanning two sessions and increasing overall costs.
Silver Amalgam Fillings:
 Amalgam fillings, while not as durable as porcelain or gold, still provide a long-lasting solution for cavity repair with high bite resistance and cost-effectiveness compared to composite resin. However, they have drawbacks such as poor aesthetic appearance, potential discoloration over time leading to a greyish tinge on surrounding teeth, and the need for significant tooth removal sometimes, risking the destruction of healthy molars.
Composite Resin Fillings:
 Composite fillings, also known as tooth-coloured fillings, are a popular choice for dental fixtures due to their ability to match the aesthetic appearance of natural teeth closely. They offer versatility, micro-mechanically attaching to peripheral tooth structure for added support without extensive preparation, allowing dentists to remove minimal parts from the affected tooth. However, these fillings are less durable than amalgam, porcelain, or gold, often requiring replacement every five years.

Are There Any Alternatives to Dental Fillings?
In cases where decayed teeth are not suitable for standard filling materials due to significant erosion from bacterial infection, dental crowns may be considered. However, dentists often opt for indirect fillings if the remaining teeth are healthy. There are two primary types of indirect fillings:
Inlays
: Similar to direct fillings, inlays eliminate cavities, with most material placed on the tooth's cusp and cemented into position.
Onlays
: Also known as partial crowns, onlays cover more than one cusp and involve more extensive application than inlays
Both inlays and onlays share similarities with composite fillings, providing a natural aesthetic appearance. They are especially suitable for cavities in front teeth. Unlike composite fillings, they offer high durability, lasting up to 30 years or more. 
The application involves two sessions.
The first phase includes cleaning, removal of decayed tooth structure, and taking an impression for customization. A temporary cover is placed during the waiting period.
The second session involves directly applying the custom-fitted filling, followed by an examination for proper alignment.
Indirect fillings excel in evenly distributing bite force across molars due to their customized fit, requiring minimal reshaping or post-procedure polishing.

Frequently Asked Questions
                     How long does a dental filling last? 
Fillings can last for several years or even more than a decade, depending on personal eating habits and the base material of the fixture. For instance, silver amalgam fillings are durable enough to last 15 years, while gold fillings go beyond that. However, it's rare to see patients opting for such variants due to their cost.
                     Are dental fillings painful?  
Fillings typically do not cause any pain during the procedure. However, there may be some discomfort if the patient has severe tooth decay. Regardless, dentists will use a numbing gel and a local anesthetic to mitigate the potential pain.
                     What should you avoid when you have a newly filled tooth? 
Dentists advise avoiding hard or chewy foods alongside any sugary or carbonated beverages. These can result in tooth decay, causing bacteria to compromise the integrity of the filling materials, which can lead to additional complications.
                     What are the most common signs of dental caries? 
The three most common signs of dental caries are persistent toothache, oral hypersensitivity, and bad breath. As such, consulting a dentist regularly can help in pre-emptively detecting such issues.
                     Is it common to experience toothache after getting a dental filling? 
Mild discomfort or tooth sensitivity is typical after such a procedure. However, this usually subsides over time. If the issue persists, consult us immediately, as it may indicate an incorrectly placed filling or accidental nerve damage.

                     How long do dental fillings last?

                     What is the procedure for dental filling at Dental Krafts?
