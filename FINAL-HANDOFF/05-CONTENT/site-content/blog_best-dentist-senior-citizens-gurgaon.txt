Common Dental Services for Seniors
Preventive Care
Preventive care is vital for seniors to avoid the risk of tooth decay, gum disease, and other oral health issues. Regular checkups and cleanings can help detect problems early and maintain a healthy mouth.
Root Canal Treatment
Root canal treatments are common among seniors, especially for those with decayed or damaged teeth. A root canal can help save a tooth that might otherwise need to be extracted, keeping your natural smile intact.
Tooth Extractions
Sometimes, teeth become so damaged that extraction is necessary. For seniors, this may be the first step toward getting dentures or implants, which can restore function and appearance.
Dentures and Implants
If you’ve lost teeth, dentures or dental implants can help restore your smile. Dentures are removable, while implants provide a permanent solution that looks and feels like natural teeth.
Tips for Maintaining Oral Health as You Age
Keeping your teeth healthy as you get older doesn’t just depend on visiting the dentist. Here are some tips to maintain good oral health:
Brush your teeth twice daily using fluoride toothpaste.
Floss regularly to remove plaque and prevent gum disease.
Use mouthwash to help reduce bacteria and prevent dry mouth.
Stay hydrated to avoid dry mouth, which can be common in seniors.
Avoid smoking, which can worsen dental problems.

What is the 2-2-2 Rule for Teeth?
The 2-2-2 rule is simple: brush your teeth twice a day for two minutes each time and visit your dentist twice a year for regular check-ups.
What is a Geriatric Dentist?
A geriatric dentist specializes in dental care for older adults, addressing age-related issues like gum disease, tooth loss, and dry mouth.
