Types of Dentures Available at Our Dental Clinic
At Dental Krafts, we stay current with dental innovations, offering top-notch products and oral care for dental implants, dentures, and crowns. Dentures and removable attachments come in various types, recommended based on a comprehensive dental assessment.
Partial Dentures
Partial dentures rely on support from gums or underlying bones to remain stable in your mouth. Notably, they have unique clasps that provide stability to artificial teeth. These dentures can be removed during sleep or for cleaning purposes. One of their key benefits is supporting the remaining natural teeth by keeping them in position and filling the spaces appropriately.
Complete Dentures
also known as Full dentures, replace missing teeth across the gum line. Dentists use a dental adhesive to secure these dentures. The main components of traditional dentures include artificial teeth and a gum-coloured base. Complete dentures can be removed for the upper or lower jaw, and materials like acrylic or porcelain are commonly used for permanent dentures.
Immediate Dentures
are temporary appliances suitable for patients who have undergone 
tooth extractions
. They serve as a temporary solution until the permanent dentures are ready, allowing sufficient time for healing from previous dental issues.
Implant-supported Dentures
Fixed dentures, acting as a non-removable bridge in your mouth, can only be removed by a dentist using a systematic procedure. However, their maintenance and cleaning requirements are higher than those of removable appliances, necessitating proper care and attention to oral hygiene.
Implant-retained Dentures
Implant-supported dentures attach to dental implants for enhanced support and stability. Removable in nature, they don't require dental glue for fitment. Dentists may recommend this type to reduce reliance on jawbone and gum tissues.

Denture Preparation Process at Dental Krafts
Whether permanent or 
partial dentures
, the manufacturing process of these substitutes must be impeccable to ensure top-quality replacements that can withstand challenging and soft foods. At Dental Krafts, we select the best denture materials and follow a stepwise procedure for denture fabrication.
Creation of Suitable Dental Impressions:
Dentists must obtain an accurate impression of your upper and lower jaw to create dental bridges. This involves taking preliminary and final impressions of your mouth, including the jawbone, gums, and teeth. This crucial step ensures the precision of the complete denture.
Making of an Accurate Dental Model:
Dental impressions are sent to the laboratory, where technicians use model types such as wax, plastic, and stone to create the preliminary model. This replica of your mouth serves as the base for crafting modern dentures.
Creation of Tailormade Denture:
In this step, the dental technician utilizes materials like acrylic, resin, or nylon to craft the replacement teeth (dentures). They create a cast of the denture, featuring a customized replica of your lower or upper jaw. The final stages involve placing and polishing artificial teeth on the denture, completing the entire process. Following this detailed procedure significantly minimizes the chances of an ill-fitting denture.
Careful Fitment of Dentures:
Similar to the attention and care during the preparation of a complete denture, the fitment requires skilful execution. At Dental Krafts, our team follows the following steps to protect your remaining natural teeth:
The dentist places the denture on your gums.
If there is excess pressure on any part of your mouth, the dentist performs minor adjustments in the denture.
Your mouth's soft tissues require sufficient time to adapt to the dentures.
According to adaptability, the dentist suggests multiple settings.
The fitment process for hybrid dentures or fixed bridges remains the same. However, some denture types do not require constant adjustments. Additionally, the requirement for denture adhesive varies from patient to patient.
Caring for Your Dentures
A partial denture is easy to clean and maintain. You can remove such attachments and clean the 
gum
 tissue easily. However, denture wearers who prefer robust fitment can opt for a conventional denture. Before you start wearing dentures, you need to know some care tips related to the base and the artificial tooth. Here are some valuable pointers that can assist you in self-care:
Keep patience at the beginning and wear dentures all the time
Use fluoride toothpaste to maintain good oral hygiene
Avoid chewing gum and eating hard food items
Use a denture brush to clean at the end of the day
Soak the dentures overnight and invest in a suitable denture cleaner
Remove food particles and debris while cleaning dentures
Chew slowly in the beginning and use both sides of your mouth
Use denture adhesives if you face shrinking of the jawbone
A soft-bristled brush proves crucial to an immediate denture or any other type. Do not fret over minor irritation in the initial days after the procedure. Also, gently brush your mouth and put less stress on your facial muscles.

When Do You Need Dentures?
Sometimes, you require potential dental solutions to rectify some oral issues. Well, dentures can become one of the preferred attachments in specific cases. Here are some of the well-known scenarios that prompt the requirement for dentures.
Chronic Toothache:
Severe tooth decay can lead to intense pain and discomfort, often necessitating treatments like root canals. In more advanced cases, the extraction of the entire tooth might be required. Dentures offer a practical solution in such situations, addressing toothache and restoring oral functionality.
Missing Teeth Due to Injuries or Accidents:
The loss of teeth can occur due to a sports injury or a traumatic accident. Eating with remaining teeth may exert undue pressure on molars, potentially leading to further issues. Dentures serve as ideal solutions, alleviating stress on other teeth and mitigating the risk of additional tooth loss. Temporary or permanent dentures can effectively address such situations.
Difficulty in Eating Certain Foods:
Difficulty eating hard food items like nuts or vegetables may be attributed to issues such as cavities, cracked teeth, or periodontal disease. Dentures offer a solution by replacing the problematic tooth and addressing difficulties in consuming certain foods. If you encounter challenges while eating, it is advisable to consult a dentist for appropriate solutions.
