Is this for you?

If any of these sound familiar.

  • Missing teeth. You've lost one or more teeth to decay, gum disease or an accident
  • Loose denture or bridge. A removable denture or bridge feels loose or uncomfortable
  • Stop teeth shifting. You want to stop neighbouring teeth from shifting into the gap
  • Bite with confidence. You'd like to bite into an apple again without thinking about it

How it works

How an implant works

A small titanium post is placed in the jawbone where the root used to be. Over 3–6 months the bone bonds to it.

A connector (abutment) and a custom crown then sit on top, so you get a tooth that stays put and doesn't need neighbouring teeth to be cut down, as a bridge does.

Your options

Ways to treat it.

Single implantOne missing tooth, one implant, one crown.
Implant bridgeSeveral missing teeth in a row, held by two or more implants.
All-on-4 / All-on-6A full set of fixed teeth on four to six implants. See the All-on-4 page.
Implant-retained dentureYour denture clicks onto two to four implants, so it no longer slips.
With bone graftingIf bone has shrunk after a long gap, it can often be rebuilt first.

Step by step

What happens, and when.

  1. Consultation & scanDr Ruchi examines you and checks bone height and width on an X-ray or 3D scan.
  2. Plan & estimateYou see where the implant will go, the timeline and what each step involves, in writing.
  3. PlacementA short procedure under local anaesthesia, usually about an hour for one implant.
  4. Healing (3–6 months)You may wear a temporary tooth. The bone bonds to the implant.
  5. Final crownWe take an impression and fit a zirconia or ceramic crown matched to your teeth.
  6. ReviewsCheck-ups and cleaning keep the implant healthy for the long term.

Before anything starts

A written plan, itemised.

After the examination you get a written plan: your options, what each step involves and how many visits it takes — itemised part by part. You decide before anything begins, and there's no pressure to start that day.

  • Options, simplest to ideal. You'll hear the honest trade-offs of each.
  • Everything in writing. Take it home, ask anything, decide when you're ready.
  • Second opinions welcome. Bring your X-rays and the plan you were given.

Aftercare

Looking after it afterwards.

Soft food and no hard chewing on that side for the first few days.
Brush and floss around the implant like a natural tooth.
Don't smoke, since it raises the risk of failure.
Come in for a check-up every 6 months.

Questions

What people ask us.

Does getting an implant hurt?

It's done under local anaesthesia. Most patients say it was easier than an extraction. Mild soreness for 2–3 days is normal, and painkillers handle it.

How long do implants last?

With good care and regular check-ups, many last 15–20 years or more. The crown on top may need replacing sooner.

Am I too old for an implant?

Age isn't the limit. Bone health and general health are. Many of our implant patients are in their 60s and 70s.

I have diabetes. Can I get an implant?

Usually yes, if it's well controlled. We'll check with you and your physician.

What decides how my implant is planned?

The implant system, the crown material, and whether you need bone grafting. Your written plan will itemise each.
