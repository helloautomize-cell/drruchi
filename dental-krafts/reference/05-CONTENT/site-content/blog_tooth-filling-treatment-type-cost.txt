What is a Tooth Filling?
    A tooth filling involves removing decayed tooth material, cleaning the affected area, and filling the cavity with a suitable material. This helps restore the tooth's shape and function and prevents further decay. If decay is severe, a 
root canal treatment
 may sometimes be recommended before filling.
Types of Fillings
Amalgam Fillings:
 Made from a mixture of metals, these are durable and cost-effective. Suitable for back teeth due to their strength.
Composite Fillings:
 Resin-based and match the color of your teeth, making them ideal for visible areas. They can complement 
dental crowns
 if needed.
Ceramic Fillings:
 Made from porcelain, these are durable and resistant to staining, perfect for visible teeth.
Glass Ionomer Fillings:
 Made from acrylic and glass, these release fluoride to protect teeth and are commonly used for fillings below the gum line. Early care like this helps prevent future 
gum disease
.

The Filling Process
Examination and Diagnosis:
 Your dentist examines your teeth and may take X-rays to determine the extent of decay.
Anesthesia:
 Local anesthesia is administered to numb the area around the affected tooth.
Decay Removal:
 The decayed part of the tooth is removed using a dental drill, laser, or air abrasion tool.
Cleaning:
 The cavity is cleaned to remove any debris or bacteria.
Filling the Cavity:
 The chosen filling material is applied in layers, with each layer being hardened.
Shaping and Polishing:
 The filling is shaped to fit the tooth and polished for a smooth finish.
Benefits of Tooth Fillings
Restores Function:
 Allows you to chew and speak normally, supporting overall oral health.
Prevents Further Decay:

dental implants

Improves Appearance:
 Especially with composite and ceramic fillings that match the tooth color, helping with a 
smile makeover
 if desired.
