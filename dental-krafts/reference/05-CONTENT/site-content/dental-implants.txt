Types of tooth implants
There are several types of dental implants, including:
Endosteal Implants
                           Commonly placed directly into the jawbone, these implants often resemble small screws, cylinders, or plates
Subperiosteal Implants
                           Positioned under the gum but not within the jawbone, this implant is suitable for those with a shallow jawbone, avoiding the need for bone augmentation
Zygomatic Implants
                           Longer implants anchored in the cheekbone (zygoma) instead of the jawbone, they are employed when severe bone loss occurs in the upper jaw
All-on-4 Implants
                           This technique strategically places four implants in the jaw to support an entire arch of teeth, commonly used for full-mouth reconstruction.
Mini Implants
                           Smaller than traditional implants, these are used in limited space or when bone quantity is insufficient for regular implants.
Immediate Load Implants
                           Same-day implants enable the placement of a temporary tooth or crown on the same day as the implant procedure.
Two-Stage Dental Implants
                           A traditional method involves placing the implant into the jawbone, covering it with gum tissue, and allowing integration. The second stage adds the abutment and crown to complete the procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions
                     Are dental implants safe?
Dental implant surgery, one of dentistry's safest procedures, uniquely stimulates natural bone beneath the missing tooth. Like any minor oral surgery, there's a slight risk of infection, inflammation, and pain, which are manageable based on your situation.
                     How long does it take?
The duration for placing dental implants varies with the technique and tooth location. Typically, the procedure lasts about one hour. Integration with the bone takes approximately 3 months for the lower jaw and 6 months for the upper jaw.
                     Are dental implants painful?
The dental implant procedure is typically not painful, as patients can be fully or partially sedated or numbed with local anesthetic. The goal is minimal discomfort. After surgery, when numbness subsides, patients may experience mild pain or discomfort, which can be managed with over-the-counter pain medication.
                     How long do the implants last?
Implants can last for many years with proper care and strong, healthy, supporting bone. Regular dental checkups are crucial to ensure their functionality, cleanliness, and overall health. Neglecting care may result in a coating similar to neglected natural teeth, leading to potential infection and bone loss.
                     What is the success rate of dental implants?
Outcomes vary with bone health, general health, oral hygiene, smoking, implant position, and follow-up care. Your dentist will discuss the benefits and risks for your individual case.
                     What are dental implants, and how do they help with missing teeth?
Dental implants are titanium posts placed into the jawbone to act as artificial tooth roots. They provide a strong foundation for replacement teeth, filling gaps left by missing teeth and restoring functionality and aesthetics.
